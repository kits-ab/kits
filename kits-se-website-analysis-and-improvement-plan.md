# KITS.se — Website Analysis and Improvement Plan

**Scope:** Greenfield website strategy for KITS, based on the public website and competitor research conducted on 10 August 2026.  
**Primary goal:** Generate more qualified B2B leads.  
**Secondary goal:** Support recruitment without letting the career journey dilute the buyer journey.

## 1. Website category and strategic role

KITS is a **Swedish specialist B2B IT-consultancy website**. Its offer sits at the intersection of system development, integration, project delivery, IT security and OT security. The intended buyers are likely technology, security and business leaders in organisations with complex or business-critical digital environments. A second audience consists of experienced technical candidates and early-career cyber talent.

The website should therefore act as a **trust and demand-generation platform**, not simply as a company brochure. Its job is to make it easy for a prospective buyer to answer four questions:

1. Can KITS solve my specific technical or security problem?
2. Why should I trust KITS with a business-critical delivery?
3. What does working with KITS look like?
4. Who should I speak to next?

The current site already has a valuable, ownable position—**“Keep IT Simple” + “Keep IT Secure”**—and an offer that spans development and cyber security. That combination should become the centre of the new experience. [KITS](https://kits.se/)

## 2. Current-site analysis

### Design

The current visual direction is clean and restrained, using a soft, nature-inspired palette and a modern technical stack. It feels calm, approachable and more personal than a typical enterprise consultancy. The site appears responsive and uses consistent page templates.

The main weakness is not basic polish; it is **commercial emphasis**. The visual hierarchy does not yet make the specialist offer, proof of competence or next action feel unavoidable. The design would benefit from stronger contrast, a more distinctive editorial art direction, more purposeful use of technical/cultural imagery, and more clearly differentiated calls to action.

### Content

The homepage states the overall offer clearly and separates the customer and career messages. Service pages list useful capabilities, including system development, integration, penetration testing and OT security. The career page contains substantial culture and Cyberakademi material. [Services](https://kits.se/tjanster/) · [Careers](https://kits.se/karriar/)

However, buyer-facing content is generally **capability-led rather than evidence-led**. It describes what KITS can do, but seldom proves delivery through customer outcomes, named experts, delivery methods, certifications, partner status, measurable results or detailed cases. The public archive also contains older blog/news content, while current insight content is not prominent in the main navigation.

### Layout and conversion

The main navigation covers Services, the two positioning pillars, Careers, KitsCon and About. Contact exists, but it is not the strongest primary action in the main journey. The homepage offers two broad routes—services and careers—then repeats contact information in the footer. [Contact](https://kits.se/kontakt/)

This creates an understandable but shallow funnel:

`Homepage → broad service overview → service description → generic contact`

For a high-consideration B2B purchase, the journey needs more decision support between service discovery and contact: relevant examples, proof, named specialists, delivery expectations and a low-friction way to start a conversation.

### Structure

The site is compact and simple to navigate, which is a strength. It is, however, organised primarily by internal company themes rather than a buyer’s decision process. For example, **KitsCon** is a top-level item despite being primarily culture/employer-brand material, while Cases, Industries, Insights and Contact are not equally prominent.

## 3. Competitor benchmark

“Better” here means **objectively stronger at observable website functions**—service discovery, trust-building, proof, editorial freshness and conversion—not a claim that these companies deliver better consulting services or that their budgets are known.

| Benchmark | Why it is relevant | Observable strengths worth adapting |
|---|---|---|
| [Omegapoint](https://www.omegapoint.se/) | Closest strategic overlap: secure digitalisation, application development and cyber security. | Clear offering/industry/insight architecture; visible client, employee and delivery metrics; customer logos; named contacts within offer areas; current expert content and events. |
| [HiQ](https://hiq.se/) | Broad Swedish/Nordic technology consultancy competing for both buyers and talent. | Deep service and industry routes; cases and knowledge hub; strong customer social proof; contact is prominent; clear separation of services, industries, knowledge and careers. |
| [Knowit](https://www.knowit.se/) | Large Nordic digitalisation and security consultancy with a mature content model. | Case-led storytelling with identifiable customer outcomes; homepage campaigns; self-assessment/lead-generation tools; strong link between content, customers and careers. |
| [Nexer](https://nexergroup.com/sv/) | Swedish technology, strategy and cyber competitor with a visible employer and content brand. | Campaign-led editorial presentation; downloadable reports; event recordings; global scale proof; distinct industry and service collections. |

KITS should not copy the competitors’ large, complex mega-menus. Its advantage can be **clarity, senior accountability and specialist depth**. The opportunity is to combine KITS’s simpler structure with the evidence, service depth and conversion discipline visible in these benchmarks.

## 4. Prioritised improvement plan

Importance reflects the needs of a specialist B2B consultancy focused on lead generation. Effort is relative: **S** = small, **M** = medium, **L** = large.

| Improvement | Why it matters for KITS | Importance | Effort |
|---|---|:---:|:---:|
| Reposition the homepage around a concise buyer promise | Lead with the business problems KITS solves and make “Simple + Secure” concrete, rather than opening with generic consultancy language. | Critical | M |
| Make contact a primary, persistent action | Add a clear “Talk to an expert” or equivalent CTA in the header, service templates and key content pages, with a short form and direct expert option. | Critical | S |
| Build customer proof | Publish approved customer logos, concise testimonials and 4–6 outcome-led case studies. This is the largest current trust gap. | Critical | L |
| Redesign service pages for decision-making | For each priority service, explain the customer problem, scope, approach, deliverables, relevant expertise/proof and a service-specific CTA. | Critical | L |
| Create a buyer-first information architecture | Use a simple top-level structure: Services, Industries, Cases & Insights, Careers, About, Contact. Keep the navigation compact. | High | M |
| Introduce priority-industry pages | Start with the sectors where KITS has credible proof—especially industrial/OT, regulated organisations and business-critical digital services. | High | M |
| Establish a stronger visual system | Retain KITS’s approachable character, but improve typography, contrast, component consistency, imagery, page rhythm and CTA visibility. | High | L |
| Surface expert authority | Introduce expert profiles, subject ownership, credentials, speaking/published work and direct routes to relevant contacts. | High | M |
| Launch an insight hub | Publish useful, current material on secure development, penetration testing, OT security, NIS2/IEC 62443 and practical AI/security topics. Promote it in navigation and service pages. | High | M |
| Turn packaged offers into conversion assets | Give Pentest, OT-security expert support and application-security training their own focused landing pages with a defined outcome and next step. | High | M |
| Improve career conversion without over-prioritising it | Show current roles, real people, culture, Cyberakademi and how technical growth works; retain a direct route to the jobs platform. | High | M |
| Reposition KitsCon | Move it under Careers or Insights unless it becomes a public, externally marketed event. Preserve it as valuable culture proof. | High | S |
| Strengthen SEO foundations | Create searchable service/industry/case/expert pages, unique metadata, internal linking and appropriate organisation, service, article and case schema. | High | M |
| Add measurement and lead operations | Track contact starts/submits, expert CTA clicks, service-page depth, case engagement and job clicks; connect qualified enquiries to a defined response process. | High | S |
| Raise accessibility and performance quality | Design and test for WCAG 2.2 AA, keyboard support, readable contrast, semantic structure, responsive media and strong Core Web Vitals. | High | M |
| Add English only when commercially justified | Add English once international sales, recruitment or partner communication has a defined owner and content-maintenance commitment. | Medium | L |

## 5. Recommended future structure

```text
Home
├── Services
│   ├── System development
│   ├── Integration
│   ├── Application security / Pentest
│   ├── OT security
│   └── Training and packaged offers
├── Industries
│   ├── Industrial and OT environments
│   ├── Regulated / public organisations
│   └── [Only sectors supported by real proof]
├── Cases & Insights
│   ├── Customer cases
│   ├── Articles and guides
│   ├── Events / KitsCon
│   └── Expert profiles
├── Careers
│   ├── Open roles
│   ├── Life at KITS
│   └── Cyberakademi
├── About KITS
└── Contact an expert
```

The homepage should act as a short, persuasive overview: clear promise, priority services, proof, selected case(s), named experts, current insight and a direct contact route. It should not try to carry every service description or all culture content.

## 6. Delivery sequence

1. **Foundation and conversion:** agree positioning, audience priorities, proof inventory, information architecture, contact experience, analytics and measurement.
2. **Core sales experience:** create the homepage, priority service templates, packaged-offer pages, customer proof and key industry pages.
3. **Brand and recruitment:** apply the visual system across the site; build careers, Cyberakademi and culture content; reposition KitsCon.
4. **Content engine and optimisation:** publish cases and insights on a sustainable cadence, monitor conversion/search data, and improve the highest-value pages continuously.

## 7. Success measures

Before launch, establish a baseline and then track:

- Qualified B2B enquiries and meetings booked from the website.
- Conversion rate from priority service and industry pages to expert contact.
- Case-study views and assisted conversions.
- Organic visibility and non-branded traffic for priority service terms.
- Engagement with insights and downloadable resources.
- Completed job applications and clicks through to the recruitment platform.
- Accessibility conformance and Core Web Vitals for high-traffic templates.

## Assumptions and safeguards

- KITS can obtain approval to publish some customer names, outcomes, quotes and partner/certification evidence. Where confidentiality prevents this, use anonymised but still quantified case studies.
- The site should be Swedish-first; English is a later commercial decision, not a default translation project.
- The new site should be deliberately simpler than the benchmark firms’ websites. A smaller consultancy wins by being easier to understand and easier to contact.
- The plan does not prescribe a CMS or implementation stack. Those choices should follow a separate content-governance and operating-model decision.
